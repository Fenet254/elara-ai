After learning HTML and CSS, I built a simple To-Do application.

Although it was basic, it introduced me to the idea that software could respond to users.

How events triggered functions.

For the first time I wasn't just writing code.

# Memory 7 — Understanding the Backend

One question continued bothering me:

How do websites remember users after they log in?

That question eventually led me to backend development.

Learning Node.js, Express, MongoDB, authentication, password hashing, and JWT answered many of the questions I had asked years earlier.

Understanding backend systems made the internet feel much less mysterious.

# Memory 8 — Discovering Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence changed my perspective on software.

Traditional programming tells computers exactly what to do.

Machine learning teaches computers using examples.

Watching models make predictions fascinated me.

It felt like discovering a completely different way of building software.

That curiosity eventually led me toward AI research and machine learning projects.

# Memory 9 — First Internship

Joining the Ethiopian Artificial Intelligence Institute was an important milestone.

I worked on an airplane weapon detection project — a computer vision system for identifying weapons from imagery and video in an aviation security context.

It was my first opportunity to experience AI development in a professional environment.

Working alongside experienced people taught me that real engineering involves much more than writing code.

It involves communication, experimentation, teamwork, and continuous learning.

---

# Memory 10 — INSA Internship

Another important milestone came during my internship at INSA.

One lesson from my mentor permanently changed how I debug software.

Instead of jumping between different fixes, we were encouraged to explain programs line by line.

At first it felt slow.

Later I realized it helped reveal mistakes much faster.

Today that habit is one of the foundations of my debugging process.

# Memory 11 — Research Thinking

Working on research-oriented AI projects introduced me to a different way of thinking.

Instead of asking,

"Does the code run?"

I started asking,

"Why does it behave this way?"

"What evidence supports this result?"

"How can I improve it?"

Research taught me to think more carefully and systematically.

Building Elara has made me reflect on my own journey more deeply than any previous project.
