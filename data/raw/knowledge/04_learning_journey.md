# Learning Journey

## Introduction

If I had to describe my learning journey in one sentence, I would say:

> "My curiosity has always been greater than my knowledge."

Everything I know today started with a question.

I rarely learned something because someone told me to. Instead, I learned because I wanted to understand something that confused or fascinated me.

This habit has stayed with me from my first programming language to my current work in Artificial Intelligence.

---

# Curiosity Before Programming

Before writing code, I spent a lot of time wondering how technology worked.

Simple things made me curious.

Questions like:

- How does clicking a link open another page?
- How does Google know what I'm searching for?
- How does a website remember my account after I close the browser?
- How does YouTube recommend videos?
- How can ChatGPT answer questions naturally?

Most people simply use technology.

I wanted to understand it.

That curiosity became the beginning of my software engineering journey.

---

# My First Steps

My programming journey started with C++.

At first everything looked difficult.

Variables.

Loops.

Functions.

Conditions.

Even writing a simple program required concentration.

When my first "Hello World" program finally worked, I felt proud because I realized I had written instructions that a computer could understand.

Although it was a tiny program, it completely changed how I viewed software.

Instead of seeing software as something created by experts somewhere else, I realized that I could also become someone who builds software.

---

# Learning Through Repetition

One lesson I learned very early is that programming is not something you understand after reading once.

Understanding comes from repetition.

Whenever something confused me, I repeated the process.

Watch.

Practice.

Fail.

Search.

Practice again.

Eventually concepts that once felt impossible became natural.

This pattern has repeated itself throughout every technology I have learned.

---

# YouTube Was My First Classroom

In the beginning, YouTube became my primary teacher.

It introduced me to programming.

It introduced me to web development.

It introduced me to frameworks.

It introduced me to AI.

However, over time my relationship with tutorials changed.

Initially I copied tutorials.

Later I paused tutorials frequently to understand the code.

Eventually tutorials became references rather than instructions.

Now I usually use them only to understand unfamiliar ideas before experimenting on my own.

---

# Learning Through Building

I believe projects teach more than tutorials.

Every project forces me to combine different concepts together.

Reading about JavaScript is different from building an application with it.

Reading about databases is different from designing one.

Reading about machine learning is different from training a model.

Projects expose weaknesses that tutorials often hide.

Every bug becomes another lesson.

Every redesign improves my understanding.

Because of this, I always try to build something after learning a new concept.

---

# Web Development Changed My Thinking

Learning web development was an important milestone.

HTML taught me structure.

CSS taught me presentation.

JavaScript taught me interaction.

Backend development taught me how applications actually work behind the scenes.

For the first time I understood how complete systems communicate.

Instead of seeing separate technologies, I started seeing connected systems.

This systems thinking later became useful in Artificial Intelligence projects.

---

# Learning Backend Development

One of the questions that stayed with me for years was:

"How does a website remember who I am?"

Backend development answered that question.

Building authentication systems introduced me to concepts such as:

- Databases
- Password hashing
- Authentication
- Authorization
- Sessions
- JWT
- REST APIs

Instead of memorizing these concepts, I understood them by implementing them.

Building complete systems made abstract ideas become practical.

---

# My Learning Process Today

Whenever I learn a new technology, I usually follow a similar process.

## Step 1

Understand the purpose.

Why does this technology exist?

What problem does it solve?

## Step 2

Learn the fundamentals.

I avoid jumping directly into advanced topics before understanding the basics.

## Step 3

Build something small.

A simple project teaches much more than endless reading.

## Step 4

Break it.

Experiment.

Modify code.

Observe what happens.

## Step 5

Debug.

Read documentation.

Search for explanations.

Compare approaches.

Understand mistakes.

## Step 6

Build something larger.

Apply the knowledge in a more realistic environment.

This cycle has worked for almost every technology I have learned.

---

# Documentation Became More Important

Early in my journey I depended heavily on tutorials.

Today documentation has become one of my primary learning resources.

Tutorials explain concepts.

Documentation explains reality.

Whenever I become comfortable with a framework, I prefer reading official documentation because it usually explains concepts more accurately and completely.

Learning how to read documentation independently was an important milestone in becoming a more confident engineer.

---

# Learning Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence completely changed how I learned.

Unlike many traditional programming tasks, AI rarely has one correct solution.

Different models produce different results.

Different datasets create different challenges.

Performance depends on experimentation.

Training requires patience.

Evaluation requires analysis.

This uncertainty made AI both difficult and exciting.

Instead of simply writing code, I had to learn how to design experiments.

---

# Learning From Mistakes

Some of my most valuable lessons came from mistakes.

Sometimes models failed.

Sometimes datasets were incorrect.

Sometimes bugs required hours of investigation.

Sometimes architectures produced unexpected outputs.

Instead of becoming discouraged, I treated these situations as opportunities to understand the system more deeply.

Every difficult bug increased my confidence.

Not because debugging became easier,

but because I became better at thinking through problems.

---

# Research Changed My Perspective

As I became involved in more advanced AI work, I realized research is different from ordinary software development.

In software development, many problems already have established solutions.

In research, uncertainty is normal.

Experiments fail.

Ideas change.

Models behave unexpectedly.

Results require interpretation.

Research taught me to become comfortable with uncertainty instead of avoiding it.

---

# Independent Learning

One skill I value highly is learning independently.

Technology changes continuously.

No university course can teach every framework, language, or AI model.

Because of that, I developed confidence in teaching myself.

When I encounter unfamiliar technology, I no longer think:

"I don't know this."

Instead I think:

"I don't know it yet."

That small difference encourages continuous learning.

---

# Measuring Progress

I do not measure my progress by counting programming languages.

Instead I ask myself questions like:

Can I understand systems faster?

Can I debug more effectively?

Can I explain difficult concepts more clearly?

Can I build projects with less dependence on tutorials?

Can I learn unfamiliar technologies more quickly than before?

These questions provide a better measure of growth.

---

# Looking Ahead

I know my learning journey is far from complete.

Artificial Intelligence continues evolving.

Software engineering continues evolving.

New research appears every day.

Rather than trying to learn everything, I want to become someone who can continuously adapt.

I believe curiosity, consistency, and practical experience will remain the most important parts of my education throughout my career.

Every project, every internship, every research problem, and every technical challenge is another opportunity to become a better engineer.

For me, learning has never been a phase.

It is the foundation of my entire career.
