# Artificial Intelligence Journey

## Introduction

Artificial Intelligence did not become my passion overnight.

It was the result of years of curiosity.

Before I learned AI, I spent years asking questions about software.

How do websites work?

How do servers communicate?

How does authentication work?

How do databases store information?

Eventually those questions changed.

Instead of asking how software works, I began asking:

How can software learn?

How can computers recognize images?

How can machines understand language?

How can a model make predictions from data?

Those questions completely changed my direction.

---

# Before Artificial Intelligence

Before entering AI, I spent most of my time learning software engineering.

I learned C++.

Then web development.

Frontend.

Backend.

Databases.

Authentication.

APIs.

Building complete applications gave me confidence as a developer.

More importantly, software engineering taught me something that later became extremely valuable in AI.

Every system has inputs.

Every system processes information.

Every system produces outputs.

Machine learning follows exactly the same principle.

The only difference is that instead of writing every rule manually, the model learns many of those rules from data.

Realizing this made AI feel much less mysterious.

---

# The Curiosity That Changed Everything

Artificial Intelligence attracted me because it answered a completely different kind of question.

Traditional software follows instructions.

Artificial Intelligence discovers patterns.

That difference fascinated me.

I wanted to understand:

Why does a model make one prediction instead of another?

How does it learn relationships?

How does it improve?

How does training actually work?

How can mathematics become intelligence?

These questions motivated me far more than simply using AI tools.

---

# The Rise of ChatGPT

When conversational AI became widely available, my curiosity increased even more.

Many people were interested in using ChatGPT.

I became interested in understanding it.

I wanted to know:

How does it understand language?

How can it generate coherent answers?

How does it remember context?

How are these models trained?

What happens before users ever ask a question?

Instead of seeing AI as magic, I wanted to understand the engineering behind it.

That curiosity continues to motivate me today.

---

# Computer Vision

Another important influence on my AI journey was computer vision.

Watching a model correctly recognize objects from images felt incredible.

For the first time I saw software making decisions based on patterns instead of manually written rules.

That experience completely changed my understanding of programming.

Instead of programming every answer,

we could train systems to discover answers.

This idea fascinated me.

---

# My First AI Projects

Like many beginners, I started with relatively small machine learning projects.

Each project introduced another concept.

Image classification.

Prediction.

Detection.

Classification.

Feature extraction.

Model evaluation.

Performance measurement.

Every project taught me something different.

Over time I became comfortable working with datasets, preprocessing, training, evaluation, and experimentation.

Those projects became stepping stones toward more advanced work.

---

# Learning Machine Learning

Machine learning required a completely different mindset from web development.

In software engineering, many bugs have clear causes.

Missing semicolon.

Wrong variable.

Incorrect API.

Machine learning is different.

Sometimes the code is correct.

The model still performs poorly.

Then the investigation begins.

Is the dataset correct?

Does the model have enough capacity?

Is the learning rate appropriate?

Is the preprocessing correct?

Should another architecture be used?

This uncertainty made AI both challenging and exciting.

---

# Learning Deep Learning

Deep learning introduced an entirely new level of complexity.

Neural networks.

Layers.

Activation functions.

Optimization.

Loss functions.

Gradient descent.

Backpropagation.

Initially these concepts seemed abstract.

Over time I began understanding how they work together.

Rather than memorizing formulas, I focused on understanding the intuition behind them.

That approach helped me learn much more effectively.

---

# My Learning Style In AI

Whenever I study Artificial Intelligence, I usually follow this process.

Understand the problem.

Understand the data.

Understand the model.

Implement.

Train.

Observe.

Evaluate.

Debug.

Improve.

Repeat.

Unlike many traditional programming tasks, AI rarely succeeds on the first attempt.

Iteration becomes part of the engineering process.

I enjoy that process.

---

# Internships Changed My Perspective

Academic learning introduced me to AI.

Professional experience showed me how AI is actually developed.

My internship at the Ethiopian Artificial Intelligence Institute exposed me to practical machine learning workflows.

Instead of simply completing assignments, I began thinking more like an engineer.

How should datasets be prepared?

How should experiments be organized?

How should results be evaluated?

How can improvements be measured?

Professional environments introduced a level of discipline that personal projects alone could not provide.

---

# Growing Through Research

Later, during my internship at INSA, my perspective expanded again.

Instead of only applying existing techniques, I became involved in more research-oriented work.

Research is different.

Sometimes there is no tutorial.

Sometimes there is no Stack Overflow answer.

Sometimes the solution has to be discovered through experimentation.

That experience taught me patience.

It also taught me to become comfortable with uncertainty.

Not every experiment succeeds.

Not every architecture performs well.

Research is often a process of eliminating incorrect ideas until better ones emerge.

---

# DeepObfusCode

One project that significantly shaped my AI journey is DeepObfusCode.

Unlike many earlier projects, this one involved much more than simply training a model.

It required understanding datasets.

Tokenization.

Sequence modeling.

Architecture design.

Memory optimization.

Debugging training pipelines.

Evaluating results.

Testing different approaches.

Working on this project helped me appreciate the difference between building AI applications and conducting AI engineering.

The project challenged both my software engineering skills and my research mindset.

It became one of the most technically demanding experiences in my journey.

---

# AI Is More Than Models

One lesson I have learned is that successful AI systems require much more than good models.

They require:

Good datasets.

Reliable preprocessing.

Efficient software.

Scalable backend systems.

Deployment.

Monitoring.

Evaluation.

Continuous improvement.

This realization strengthened my appreciation for software engineering.

AI and software engineering are not separate.

The strongest AI systems are built on strong engineering foundations.

---

# Debugging AI

Debugging machine learning systems is one of the most interesting parts of AI.

Sometimes the model trains correctly.

The predictions are wrong.

Sometimes the architecture is correct.

The data is incorrect.

Sometimes the dataset is good.

The preprocessing introduces errors.

Sometimes everything appears correct.

Performance still remains poor.

These situations require systematic investigation.

This kind of debugging strengthened my patience and analytical thinking.

---

# Why I Enjoy AI

Artificial Intelligence combines many things I enjoy.

Programming.

Mathematics.

Research.

Problem solving.

Experimentation.

Continuous learning.

Creativity.

Engineering.

Very few fields combine all of these together.

That is one reason I continue investing most of my learning into AI.

---

# How AI Changed Me

Learning Artificial Intelligence changed more than my technical skills.

It changed how I think.

I became more comfortable with uncertainty.

More willing to experiment.

More interested in research.

More patient when solving difficult problems.

I stopped expecting immediate success.

Instead, I learned to appreciate gradual improvement.

That mindset now influences every technical project I build.

---

# My Long-Term Direction

Artificial Intelligence is the area where I want to continue growing throughout my career.

I am especially interested in:

Natural Language Processing.

Large Language Models.

Transformer architectures.

Code intelligence.

AI-assisted software engineering.

Machine learning infrastructure.

Research engineering.

I want to continue building systems that combine strong software engineering principles with intelligent models.

My goal is not only to use Artificial Intelligence.

I want to understand it deeply enough to contribute to its development.

---

# Final Thoughts

Looking back, I realize my journey into AI was never about following trends.

It was simply the next stage of my curiosity.

Years ago I asked:

How do websites work?

Today I ask:

How can machines understand language?

Tomorrow the questions will probably become even more difficult.

I hope they do.

Because every difficult question becomes another opportunity to learn.

As long as I remain curious, I believe I will continue growing—not only as an AI engineer, but as a lifelong learner.
