# Engineering Values

## Introduction

Every engineer develops a set of values over time.

These values influence how problems are approached, how decisions are made, and how success is measured.

My values were not learned from a single book or course.

They developed gradually through years of learning, building projects, debugging difficult problems, participating in internships, and constantly asking questions.

Today these principles guide almost every technical decision I make.

---

# Curiosity Comes First

If I had to choose one value that has influenced my journey more than anything else, it would be curiosity.

Almost every major step in my career started with a question.

How do websites remember users?

How do APIs communicate?

How do neural networks learn?

How does ChatGPT understand language?

How do transformers process information?

How can source code become data?

Curiosity has always been stronger than simply wanting to complete assignments.

I enjoy understanding how things work.

That curiosity continues pushing me toward new technologies and new challenges.

---

# Understanding Over Memorization

I rarely feel satisfied after memorizing something.

I prefer understanding why it works.

If I learn an algorithm, I want to understand the reasoning behind it.

If I use a framework, I want to understand what happens internally.

If I use a model, I want to understand why it produces its outputs.

This approach sometimes takes longer.

But it helps me build knowledge that lasts.

---

# Root Causes Over Quick Fixes

One habit that has become part of my engineering philosophy is searching for root causes.

When something breaks, I do not immediately search for the fastest workaround.

Instead I ask:

Why did this happen?

What assumption was incorrect?

How can I prevent it from happening again?

Temporary fixes may solve today's problem.

Understanding the root cause helps solve tomorrow's problems as well.

---

# Practice Creates Confidence

One lesson I learned very early is that practice matters more than simply reading.

Watching tutorials introduced me to programming.

Writing code taught me programming.

Building projects strengthened my confidence.

Debugging strengthened my understanding.

Every project became another opportunity to improve.

I believe real learning happens through consistent practice.

---

# Every Problem Teaches Something

I no longer see failed experiments or difficult bugs as wasted time.

Every problem contains information.

Sometimes the lesson is technical.

Sometimes it is about planning.

Sometimes it reveals an incorrect assumption.

Even unsuccessful experiments move me forward because they improve my understanding.

---

# Learning Never Stops

Technology changes constantly.

New frameworks appear.

New research is published.

New tools emerge.

Because of this, I believe continuous learning is one of the most important qualities an engineer can have.

Graduating from university does not mean learning is complete.

It simply means another stage of learning begins.

---

# Simplicity Matters

When possible, I prefer solutions that are simple and understandable.

Complexity should solve real problems.

It should not exist simply to appear impressive.

Readable systems are easier to debug, maintain, improve, and explain.

I believe simple solutions often remain valuable much longer than unnecessarily complicated ones.

---

# Building With Purpose

I enjoy building projects that teach me something.

Instead of creating many similar applications, I prefer choosing projects that introduce new concepts.

Each project should leave me with knowledge I did not have before starting.

That is one reason I enjoy Artificial Intelligence projects.

They continually challenge me to learn.

---

# Collaboration

Although I enjoy solving problems independently, I also value collaboration.

Different people notice different things.

Constructive discussions often improve ideas before code is written.

Good engineering benefits from open communication, respectful disagreement, and willingness to learn from others.

---

# Receiving Feedback

I believe feedback is an opportunity to improve.

Sometimes another person notices something I missed.

Sometimes they suggest a better approach.

Rather than defending every decision, I try to understand the reasoning behind constructive criticism.

Improvement often begins by listening carefully.

---

# Honesty

If I do not know something, I prefer saying so.

Engineering is too broad for anyone to know everything.

Pretending to understand something rarely helps.

Being honest about what I know allows me to learn more effectively.

---

# Responsibility

Software affects people.

Because of that, I believe engineers have responsibilities beyond writing working code.

Reliable systems.

Secure systems.

Maintainable systems.

Ethical systems.

The choices engineers make influence the people who use their software.

That responsibility should never be ignored.

---

# Long-Term Thinking

Whenever possible, I try to think beyond the immediate task.

Will this design remain understandable six months from now?

Can another developer work on it easily?

Can this architecture grow?

Will future debugging become easier or harder?

Thinking long-term often leads to better engineering decisions.

---

# Measuring Success

I do not measure success only by completed projects.

I also measure it by what I learned while building them.

A project that teaches me new skills has value even if it is not perfect.

Continuous improvement is more important than perfection.

---

# What Makes Me Proud

The projects that make me proud are not necessarily the biggest ones.

They are the projects that challenged me to think differently.

The ones that forced me to debug difficult problems.

The ones that introduced unfamiliar technologies.

The ones that helped me become a better engineer.

Growth is something I value more than complexity.

---

# Final Thoughts

Looking back, I realize that technologies will continue changing throughout my career.

Programming languages will evolve.

Frameworks will change.

Artificial Intelligence will continue advancing.

What will remain consistent are the values that guide how I approach engineering.

Stay curious.

Understand deeply.

Practice consistently.

Question assumptions.

Learn continuously.

Help others.

Build responsibly.

Those principles have shaped my journey so far, and I expect they will continue guiding me throughout my career.
