# Recruiter FAQ

---

# Tell me about yourself.

I'm Fenet Gizaw, a fifth-year Software Engineering student at Jimma University with a strong interest in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and backend engineering.

I started programming because I was curious about how technology worked. Questions like "How does a website remember me after I log in?" and "How do links open other websites?" eventually led me into web development. After building a solid software engineering foundation, I shifted toward AI because I wanted to understand how computers learn from data rather than simply follow instructions.

Today I enjoy building intelligent systems, researching new ideas, and solving challenging engineering problems. My internships at the Ethiopian Artificial Intelligence Institute and INSA strengthened both my technical skills and my research mindset.

# What are you currently focusing on?

Right now my primary focus is Artificial Intelligence, especially Natural Language Processing, Large Language Models, Transformer architectures, and AI-assisted software engineering.

I'm particularly interested in systems that combine strong software engineering practices with machine learning.

---

# Why did you move from web development into AI?

Web development taught me how software works.

Artificial Intelligence taught me how software can learn.

As I became more comfortable building applications, I became increasingly curious about machine learning models, computer vision, language models, and prediction systems.

Rather than replacing software engineering, AI expanded it.

# How do you learn new technologies?

I usually follow this process:

Understand the problem.

Study the documentation.

Watch technical explanations if necessary.

Build a small project.

Experiment.

Debug.

Improve.

Finally, apply what I learned in a larger project.

Building is how I learn best.

---

# Describe your debugging process.

I begin by reproducing the issue.

Then I isolate the failing component.

I inspect inputs and outputs.

I read logs.

I follow the data through the system.

I create hypotheses instead of guessing.

Only after understanding the root cause do I implement a solution.

One practice that strongly influenced me came from my mentor during my internship at INSA. He required us to explain code line by line during presentations. That habit stayed with me and continues to influence how I debug complex systems today.
