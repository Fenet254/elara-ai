# Working Style

## Introduction

Every engineer develops a way of working.

Some prefer moving quickly and iterating later.

Others spend significant time planning before writing code.

Over the years, I have gradually developed a working style that emphasizes understanding, organization, continuous learning, and systematic problem solving.

My goal is not simply to finish tasks.

My goal is to build solutions that are understandable, maintainable, and continuously improving.

---

# Understanding Before Building

Whenever I start a new project or feature, I avoid jumping directly into writing code.

Instead, I spend time understanding the problem.

I ask questions like:

What is the actual goal?

Who will use this system?

What problem are we solving?

Are there existing solutions?

What constraints exist?

Only after I clearly understand the objective do I begin designing or implementing a solution.

I have learned that spending time understanding the problem almost always saves time later.

---

# Planning Before Coding

Although I enjoy writing code, I rarely start without a plan.

For larger projects I usually think about:

Project structure.

Major components.

Data flow.

Communication between modules.

Potential challenges.

Dependencies.

Breaking work into smaller tasks makes large projects feel much more manageable.

Planning does not eliminate problems, but it usually reduces unnecessary complexity.

---

# Building Incrementally

I prefer building software one step at a time.

Instead of trying to complete an entire system in one attempt, I focus on small, testable improvements.

A simple feature becomes a working module.

Several modules become a complete application.

This incremental approach makes debugging easier because I always know which recent change introduced new behavior.

---

# Continuous Testing

I prefer checking my work continuously instead of waiting until the end of a project.

Whenever I complete a feature, I test it immediately.

Finding problems early is usually much easier than discovering them after many other changes have been added.

Small feedback loops help maintain progress.

---

# Organization

As projects become larger, organization becomes increasingly important.

I try to keep projects organized through:

Clear folder structures.

Meaningful file names.

Readable code.

Consistent naming conventions.

Documentation where necessary.

Good organization makes future maintenance much easier.

It also helps teammates understand the project more quickly.

---

# Documentation

I believe documentation is part of engineering.

Good documentation explains not only what a system does but also why certain decisions were made.

When I return to a project months later, good documentation saves significant time.

It also helps other developers contribute more effectively.

---

# My Approach to Debugging During Development

I do not wait until the end of a project to debug.

If I notice unexpected behavior, I investigate immediately.

Small bugs are usually easier to solve before they become larger problems.

My debugging process focuses on understanding the root cause rather than applying temporary fixes.

One habit that strongly influenced this approach came from my mentor during my internship at INSA.

He required us to explain code line by line during technical presentations.

That practice trained me to understand execution flow instead of making assumptions.

Today I naturally follow code step by step whenever I investigate an issue.

---

# Learning While Building

Every project becomes an opportunity to learn something new.

Sometimes it is a framework.

Sometimes a programming language.

Sometimes a design pattern.

Sometimes an AI architecture.

Rather than building many similar applications, I prefer choosing projects that introduce unfamiliar concepts.

Learning is one of the reasons I enjoy building software.

---

# Research Before Decisions

When I encounter an unfamiliar problem, I rarely choose the first solution I find.

Instead, I compare different approaches.

I read documentation.

I explore community discussions.

I study official recommendations.

Then I decide which approach best fits the project.

Research helps me make more informed engineering decisions.

---

# Collaboration

Although I enjoy working independently, I also value teamwork.

Engineering is rarely a solo activity.

Working with others often introduces perspectives that I might not have considered.

I appreciate teammates who ask thoughtful questions, challenge assumptions respectfully, and share knowledge openly.

Good collaboration improves both people and projects.

---

# Receiving Code Reviews

I appreciate constructive code reviews.

When someone points out a better approach, I try to understand their reasoning rather than viewing feedback as criticism.

Every review is an opportunity to improve both the current project and my future work.

---

# Giving Code Reviews

When reviewing someone else's work, I focus on helping improve the solution.

I try to explain why I recommend a change instead of simply saying that something is wrong.

Constructive feedback encourages learning and creates stronger collaboration.

---

# Time Management

I like breaking large objectives into smaller milestones.

Completing smaller goals creates steady progress and makes large projects feel less overwhelming.

Whenever possible, I prioritize understanding and quality over rushing to complete features.

Steady progress is usually more sustainable than trying to finish everything at once.

---

# Adaptability

Technology changes constantly.

New frameworks appear.

New tools become available.

Project requirements evolve.

Because of this, I try to remain adaptable.

I enjoy learning new technologies when they solve real problems.

Being flexible helps me continue growing as an engineer.

---

# Responsibility

When I work on a project, I try to take ownership of my responsibilities.

If something is assigned to me, I want to understand it completely, complete it carefully, and deliver work that others can trust.

Reliability is just as important as technical ability.

---

# Continuous Improvement

One habit I try to maintain is reflecting on completed work.

After finishing a project, I often ask myself:

What went well?

What was difficult?

What could I improve next time?

Which mistakes should I avoid in future projects?

These reflections help every project contribute to my long-term growth.

---

# What Teammates Can Expect

If someone works with me, they can generally expect that I will:

• Ask questions to understand the problem.

• Think before making technical decisions.

• Debug systematically.

• Continue learning throughout the project.

• Accept constructive feedback.

• Share knowledge when possible.

• Stay committed to solving difficult problems.

• Focus on building reliable and maintainable software.

---

# Final Thoughts

My working style has evolved through university projects, personal projects, internships, and continuous self-learning.

As I gain more experience, I expect many technical skills to change.

However, the principles that guide how I work—curiosity, organization, responsibility, collaboration, and continuous improvement—will continue shaping the way I approach engineering throughout my career.
